## Ryder (3556 words)

Never trust a witch.I’d heard it a thousand times, etched into alley walls and whispered over broken bottles. I knew better. Gods, I knew. But desperation makes philosophers out of fools and fools out of everyone else—and I was already halfway down the drain when she found me.

She came cloaked in charm, her words velvet promises. Her hair darker than a raven. Her eyes the deepest purple I had ever seen.

The kind of beautiful that screamed don’t touch—but I’ve never been good at walking away from sharp edges. Not when they glint like hope.

When someone offers the thing you’ve been bleeding for, starving for, burning for, you don’t pause to check the price tag. You grab the gift with both hands and pray the cost doesn’t kill you first.

Now here I was, sprinting through the slums of New Seattle, soaked to the bone and slick with panic. The box housing this magical item pulsed in my bag like a second heartbeat. Each throb left a taste in my mouth—metallic, electric. Like chewing on a live wire.

Hot. 

Hungry.

Wrong. 

Two cybered-up orc stormed the street behind me, each step a metallic drumbeat of doom.

In New Seattle, you learned quick—don’t look a gang-orc in the eyes, don’t owe one a favor, and if they come running, run faster. Whatever the hell this thing was, it better be worth it.

I ducked under a rust-eaten fire escape, the metal bleeding rust flakes onto my jacket. Nearly clipped my skull on a dangling pipe wrapped in greasy tape, vaulting a toppled trash unit that stank like old grease and rot—undercity perfume, sharp enough to make my eyes water even though I’d lived with it for years.

Rain sheeted from rooftops like the city itself was trying to drown me. My boots skidded on wet concrete—ankle jolting, balance lurching—nearly went down.

No time. No breath. Just run.

Up ahead, neon slashed the dark—The Rusted Halo, a dive bar carved into a forgotten basement. Its red glow bled across the alley like a warning or an invitation. Here, those two were usually the same thing. The trick was knowing which you’d regret more. 

I lunged for the door, yanked it open, and spilled inside in a tangle of limbs and adrenaline.

The air hit like a fist—beer, sweat, hot grease, cheap synth-smoke. A bell above the door gave a traitorous jingle.

A few patrons looked up, mildly curious as I entered, but upon seeing no badges dismissed my appearance and went back to their drinks and conversations.

 Good. No cameras. Better.

I kept my head down, slunk past a cracked pool table and a synth-metal anthem that rattled the floors. Found a corner booth where the light didn’t quite reach and melted into it like a stain. Hat low. Head lower. I could’ve been anyone: a burnout, a grifter, just another corpse-in-progress with nothing left to lose.

Luck’s never been my thing.

Not five minutes later, the pair of orcs stormed through the bar door like wrecking balls made flesh. Rain still streamed off them in sheets, dripping onto the floorboards in small puddles. Their eyes swept the room in sharp, predatory arcs—nostrils flaring, jaws set.

Hounds with murder in their veins.

Cold purple cybernetics pulsed beneath their skin, the same glow etched in the twin vertical marks burning faint neon across their foreheads. Veins of light flickered under storm-gray skin like electric scars. Not the cheap kind of implants either—these were battlefield-grade mods. The sort that let you run through a hailstorm without slowing down and tear steel doors off hinges for warm-up.

The first one moved like a charging thunderbolt—hair slicked back, fangs bared, a dagger already half-drawn. Gold detection lenses in her eyes swept the shadows, picking up heat signatures like they were breadcrumbs.

Her partner was broader, wearing only a soaked white tank that clung to cyber-reinforced muscle. He carried a blade slung over his shoulder as if it weighed nothing, jaw tight with the kind of irritation that usually ended with someone getting ventilated through a wall.

They weren’t just hunting.

They were locked on.

I slid out of the booth like smoke, casual as a ghost. No sudden moves. Just another patron with a bladder problem. I angled toward the hallway near the restrooms, each step slow, deliberate.

Maybe they won’t clock me. Maybe they’ll think I’m nobody. Maybe this ends without my guts on the floor.

Please don’t recognize me.

I reached the end of the hall and veered hard left—shoulder to the door, burst into the alley like a bullet.

“HEY! YOU!” Their voices were deep, gravely, sounded like they went through at least two synth-smoke packs in a day.

Shit.

I ran.

Boots splashed through puddles, heart hammering like a riot drum. 

I didn’t look back. 

Didn’t need to. Their footsteps splashed in the water, close—metal scraping asphalt, hot breath heavy with violence.

I shot around a corner, yanked open a warped service door—abandoned storage room. Empty crates. Busted broom. No exit. No cover.

Eyes scanned. Fire escape.

Too high.

Double shit.

Backed up. Breathed once. Sprint. Leap.

Fingers caught the lowest rung by inches—steel slicing palm, shoulder detonating in white-hot pain. I grunted, hauled myself up like a drowning man grabbing shore.

The door behind me exploded open.

Gunfire. One round sizzled past my ear, close enough to take a piece of my soul.

I flinched, ear ringing from the sound.

I rolled onto the rooftop, lungs heaving, shoulder a mess of agony. No time to check the damage. 

Kept low. Kept moving. 

Gunfire cracked again, chasing me across tar and gravel. 

For a second I was back on the docks at thirteen, jumping crate to crate to keep from freezing. Only then, the fall would’ve just broken my legs. Now it would get me killed.

I vaulted the ledge.

Hit the next roof hard—knees screaming, boots skidding across loose gravel. Tucked. Rolled. Came up with a curse caught between my teeth.

Move.

Don’t stop.

Don’t die.

The city blurred around me—concrete and steel and too much haze and rain. I sprinted across the rooftop, lungs burning, blood pounding in my ears.

Another gap. Wider this time. I didn’t think—I just ran.

Leapt.

Cleared it by inches, landed rough on the edge of a half-finished high-rise. Barely missing the rebar jutted like ribs from the floor. Water sloshing around me.

Construction netting snapped in the wind. I ducked under a low beam and kept going.

Rooftop to rooftop.

Apartment blocks, corporate shells, skeletal towers clawing at the clouds. Neon lighting everything below in flickering colors, hot pinks and sickly greens

My legs were fire, lungs raw. Vents blurred past. Ducts, drone lights blinking overhead like silent watchers. 

For a heartbeat, I wasn’t running.

I was alive.

Felt like the old days. Ones that occasionally landed me on watch lists. Felt I was 

Ten blocks later, I collapsed behind a rusted-out AC unit. The metal was freezing—damp cold bit into my back and turned sweat to ice. 

I lay there, counting heartbeats, tracking shadows. No sirens. No boots. Just wind. Distant traffic. Somewhere below, a dog barked like it knew my sins. 

The sound followed me down the alley, sharp and accusing, until the rain drowned it out.

I think I lost them.

Eventually, I slipped off the roof and bled back into the undercity. Alleyways that didn’t ask questions. Cracks where the city forgot names, faces, lives. Neon flickered overhead like a dying memory. A man cursed out a vending machine. Somewhere, someone laughed.

The world kept spinning.

I took the long way home.

By the time I stepped through the apartment door, I was soaked, bloodied, and riding the tail-end of an adrenaline crash. My nerves were shot. My shoulder throbbed like it had a heartbeat of its own—sharp and insistent. Rain dripped from my coat, pooling on the scuffed floorboards.

Vesper was waiting.

She stood in the doorway like she’d been there for hours, chewing the inside of her cheek the way she used to when Mom came home late. Green eyes flaring—not with rage, but something worse. Fury shaped by fear. The kind of anger that only comes from love pushed to the edge and left there too long.

“Where the hell have you been?”

Her voice cracked through the silence, brittle and burning. She looked so damn small—small enough that some part of me still wanted to tell her it was going to be okay—even if we both knew it wasn’t. 

Barefoot on cracked linoleum, hoodie swallowing her like armor she didn’t know how to take off. But the fire in her stare could’ve dropped me where I stood. 

I’d taken hits that hurt less.

I pushed a hand through my soaked hair, winced when it scraped over the cut at my temple. “Vez, I’m fine. I was just… trying to make us money.”

The words felt thin the moment they left my mouth, already collapsing under their own weight.

She didn’t move. Just stood there in the kitchen doorway, blinking too fast, like she was trying to keep the tears from showing. Her jaw worked, tight and stubborn, same as when she was twelve and wouldn’t admit she’d been scared.

When she spoke, it was quiet—small enough I almost missed it.

“Javara made dinner.”

Then she turned and walked away. No storming off, no slammed doors—just her shoulders curling inward like my absence had folded her in half.

I stayed there too long, dripping on the floor, the smell of warm food drifting in from the kitchen. My stomach growled, but my throat stayed locked.

“I’ll eat later,” I muttered, and stepped into the hallway, brushing past her like we were still kids in the undercity, shoulder to shoulder in a space too small for both of us.

 I knew I was in the wrong and was hesitating and hated myself for it.

I tousled Vesper’s hair as I passed her in the hallway—gentle, familiar. The same way I used to when we were kids running wild through undercity corridors with scraped knees and stolen snacks. 

She didn’t stop me. Didn’t duck away. Just stood there, frozen in the half-light like she’d already written me off. Which stung, just a bit.

But the box tugged me away, my need for answers overriding my better judgment.

My room hadn’t changed. The chipped gray walls still peeled like sunburned skin. The blinds hung crooked, one slat always bent inward like it was spying on me. The mattress sagged in the middle, spine-wrecker that it was, draped in the same threadbare blanket I’d had since I was sixteen.

But here, at least, I was alone.

Safe. For now.

I dropped onto the bed with a wince and pulled the box from my bag, setting it gently on the blanket like it might decide to bite. Dark wood—mahogany, maybe—smelling faintly of smoke and air that hadn’t moved in years. Its surface was polished to a dull sheen, smooth as skin, interrupted only by the carvings: intricate, angular, winding like veins. A language I didn’t recognize.

And I know a lot of languages. Which meant this was either very old, very rare, or very bad.

It thrummed under my fingers—a low, rhythmic vibration that didn’t belong to wood or metal. It felt…alive. Hungry. My pulse tried to match it, traitor that it was.

Locked, of course.

I reached into the drawer for my kit, the cracked leather case soft with age and use. Tools I’d held through cold nights and worse jobs. I didn’t think. Just let my hands move, muscle memory steering while my mind drifted.

Click. 

Click. 

Too tight. 

Curse. 

Adjust. 

Try again.

The mechanism was old, but clever. Not brute-force. This lock wanted finesse, wanted a conversation. I’d had conversations like that before—late-night trades in back alleys, both sides pretending they weren’t lying.

Sweat beaded at my temple. My breath went shallow, like even air might throw it off. One pick bent too far and snapped. I swallowed the snarl, swapped it out.

Then—

Click.

The lid creaked open, hinges dragging like they hadn’t moved in decades, coughing up the scent of something sealed and forgotten. The sound set my teeth on edge. I’d oil it later—assuming I lived long enough to bother.

Dead center on the velvet lay a plate slightly bigger than my hand, its outline tapering into points like it had once been part of something much larger—a fragment that still remembered the whole it had been torn from. 

The fabric beneath it rippled in the faint draft, but the plate didn’t so much as quiver, as though its weight existed in a different direction than gravity.

The surface caught the dim light in a way that wasn’t reflection at all—more like the shimmer you glimpse in the corner of your vision when you’re certain someone’s watching, only to turn and find nothing there. The shimmer seemed alive, never quite repeating itself, like it was testing how far it could move before I truly noticed.

Its color refused to settle. Pale silver one moment, storm-grey the next, streaked with threads that pulsed faintly in the periphery—hairline seams too precise for cracks, too deliberate to be flaws. Up close, the texture was stone-smooth, as if the skin of a cliff had been worn down by a thousand tides. But there was depth—layers stacked beneath the surface, like pages pressed in a book whose words you couldn’t yet read.

It curved subtly, the way armor does when it’s shaped for something alive, but not necessarily human. The edges were clean without being sharp—organic, as though it had grown into that shape the way leaves learn their edges in spring.

When my fingers brushed it, the cold bypassed skin entirely, threading straight into bone. Something inside it stirred—not in surprise, but as if acknowledging me. The thrum that followed was steady, slow, and impossibly patient, the heartbeat of a creature that had never known fear… or perhaps had no need for it. It weighed almost nothing, light in a way that felt wrong, as though the substance had been scooped out and replaced with the memory of air under wings.

I picked up my phone—an old VeriSynth, slow but stubborn, a relic from before upgrades became mandatory—and snapped a shot. The shutter lagged, like it didn’t want to commit the thing to memory. Still, with a flick of my wrist, the image leapt to my holo-com.

I dragged it into the scanner and hit Search. The interface inhaled—lines of light blooming outward in concentric rings, a digital spiderweb seeking vibrations in the dark. The algorithms chewed. Data gnawed. Code whispered to code.

The result blinked into existence in polite, tidy letters.

DRAGON SCALE.

Below it, the info feed began to populate—links from dead forums, half-broken videos, archived darknet threads with timestamps older than me. Titles like Sky-Burners: The Government’s Biggest Cover-Up and What the Corporates Don’t Want You to Know. Grainy images of impossible wingspans, blurry close-ups of scales that looked nothing like mine.

I scrolled. It only got worse—rants about ancient wars fought above the clouds, “eyewitness accounts” from people with usernames like FireDrake420. Not a single credible archive, not even a pay walled academic paper. Just noise.

And yet, a few of the descriptions… lined up. The shimmer. The cold. The weight—or lack of it. Enough to make me close the window before I started thinking too hard.

The dragons from those half-heard stories wore colors you couldn’t mistake: red like arterial blood, black like a night stripped of stars, green like poison left to curdle, blue with storms tangled in their bellies, gold for the ones older than empires. Platinum? That wasn’t in any version I’d ever heard. Which meant either I was chasing ghosts… or I was holding one.

My fingers hovered over the keys. I was halfway through typing platinum dragon, half curious to see what the lunatics online said about that when I was interrupted by a sharp, hollow rhythm rattled my bedroom door—knuckles on wood, the kind of knock that carried no patience.

My heart leapt into my throat.

I slammed the lid shut with a muffled thunk, shoving the box deeper onto the bed like it was leaking radiation. Two strides and I was at the door. I cracked it open.

Javara.

Arms folded, jaw set in granite. His dark eyes—my eyes—held that familiar cocktail of suspicion and disappointment, stirred just for me.

“I’m busy.” I said.

“No.” His voice was flat as slate. “You’re not.”

His boot slid into the gap before I could slam the door. Smooth. Practiced. Infuriating. Then he was inside, brushing past like the room already belonged to him. He was a year younger, but you’d never know it from the way he moved—like he owned the building, like I was some squatter he was moments from evicting, like every choice I made went straight onto a list he’d keep in his pocket until the day he needed to bury me with it.

“You’ve been gone all day,” he said, his voice tight—the kind of tight that holds back an avalanche. “And lately? You’ve been gone all the damn time.”

I turned away from him, back toward the bed—toward the thing resting there, pulsing in the dim like a second heart. “I’m trying to keep us alive.”

“By shutting us out?” His words snapped like dry twigs. He stepped in, hand shooting out to clamp my arm—fingers rough, insistent, dragging me into his orbit. “You need to listen, just for once.”

I yanked free on reflex. Pain exploded down my shoulder—white-hot, electric, a jagged bolt through the old wound that never stopped waiting for moments like this. My knees buckled, the floor tilting under me, breath locked in my chest. The room went narrow, my vision tunneling until there was only him and the ache.

That injury—that night—flared in full, cruel memory.

“Now’s not a good time,” I said, voice low, steady by force alone. I kept my gaze fixed anywhere but his. If I looked, he’d see too much.

“It’s never a good time with you,” he barked. “Can’t you see we need you?”

That was it.

I spun, fists clenched, fire rising fast in my chest. “I’ve been trying, Jav! I’m out there bleeding for this family—dodging bullets, crawling through gutters, cutting deals with monsters just to keep food on the table!”

“Bullshit.” The word came out like shrapnel—fast, hot, sharp. “You’re not doing this for us. You’ve been chasing a miracle. Ever since that job five years ago—ever since you got hurt—it’s all you care about.”

“Damn right I care!” My voice cracked, raw and loud. “No one hires a cripple! We can’t afford a back-alley healer, and the licensed ones want a month’s pay just to look! I can’t work with a dead arm!”

His voice softened, but not in a kind way. “You need to let it go.”

I turned away before the next words could break something in me.

“Screw you.”

Silence.

Then—behind me—the faintest shift. Clothes brushing cloth. A foot on warped floorboards.

“This is why, isn’t it?” he said, low now. Knife-sharp. “Why you disappeared today.”

I turned just in time to see him move—fast, reckless, reaching. He lunged past me and snatched the box off the bed like it was his right, like I wasn’t standing two feet away.

“Give it back!” I surged forward, but he stepped back with a dancer’s grace, holding it high above his head. Like we were kids again. Like I was too short, too slow, too less.

“Not until you spend five goddamn minutes with your family!” he shouted, and this time his voice broke for real. Not rage. Hurt. The kind that doesn’t bleed—it leaks. Pain twisted up with betrayal, years of it.

I grabbed for the box again. He jerked it away.

The lid slipped.

The sound that followed wasn’t sound. It was presence.

A hum bloomed from the box—low and dense, like the first growl of a predator in the dark. It didn’t come from outside but from within us—inside the bones, behind the eyes. A frequency too old to be measured, too real to ignore.

The air grew thick. Charged. Metallic on the tongue. Like a storm was building inside the room, pulling energy from the walls, from us.

We both staggered. The box pulsed. The hum deepened.

I reached again, instinct overriding logic, fear overriding pain.

And then—

BOOM.

The air split.

A shock wave detonated in the center of the room. Time shattered.

The windows exploded inward, glass turning to razors mid-flight. Shards screamed across the room, slicing fabric, skin, silence.

The lights died in a single blink. Sparks showered the floor as the last bulb burst in a pop of white fire.

Everything went dark.

I threw my arms up. Felt the bite—glass ripping across skin, blood blooming hot against cold air.

Then—impact.

I slammed into the wall with the force of a wrecking ball. My back hit, then my head. Pain bloomed like fireworks behind my eyes. The bag flew from my hands. The room spun. The box hit the floor.

Then—nothing.

Silence swallowed the world. Not quiet.

Absence.

I collapsed into it.

Into the dark.
