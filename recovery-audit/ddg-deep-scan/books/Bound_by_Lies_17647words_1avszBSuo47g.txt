I threw back the last of the drink, the liquid fire scorching its way down my throat, but it did nothing to dull the ache. The walls and the party felt like they were closing in, the air too thick to breathe. I pushed up from the table, the need to escape it all clawing at me.

Slipping through the crowd like a ghost, unnoticed in the sea of celebration. I wiped at the corner of my eyes with my sleeve, my breath catching in my throat. No one even spared a glance in my direction.

I would think someone might pause, might question the best man tearing up at his  friend’s wedding, but laughter and music drowned out the cracks in my composure.

I shoved the balcony doors open, the crisp autumn air slapping against my face, sharp and unforgiving. It It grounded me for a moment bringing me back.

Emotion wasn’t normally built into us. Trained out of us by our fathers at a young age. Yet my grip on it was slipping. I was struggling to keep the mask of self-control intact.

The soft creak of the doors behind me broke through my thoughts. I didn’t have to turn around to know who it was—the familiar sound of the footsteps of none other than the groom himself.

It wasn’t a surprise, not really—I had known from the start that Angela’s heart had never truly been mine, only resting with me as a second choice. When George finally turned his gaze toward her, she hadn’t even paused. She walked away without a glance. I’d been nothing more than a placeholder.

“You make me sick,” I snarled, the bitterness of the words burning on my tongue.

George didn’t flinch; his eyes gleamed with an unsettling calm as a smirk crept across his face. “Pity,” he murmured, his tone smooth, laced with a quiet menace. “This would be a good moment to reconsider.” He leaned in, his voice dropping, each word dripping with threat. “I’m wrapping this city around my finger. Trust me, you don’t want to be on the wrong side when that happens.”

I held his gaze, unmoved, letting his attempt at intimidation wash over me like a faint breeze. George could play at being powerful, but I knew him better than anyone—and I knew where to hit.

“You don’t scare me, George,” I said slowly. Carefully. “I know every secret you think you’ve buried.” I let the threat hang in the air, then turned away. “This is a debt,” I called over my shoulder, my tone calm, chilling. “And when I come to collect, you’ll pay every last bit.”
